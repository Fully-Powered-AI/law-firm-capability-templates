# Hearing preparation pack

Before a set hearing, the arguing attorney has one draft pack: the hearing as the notice states it, the docket history, each order's directives quoted, each witness's prior statements with page cites, exhibit and checklist status as the firm records them, and an open-items list. The attorney decides every argument.

*Workflow template · Law firms · Legal and contracts · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/hearing-preparation-pack/)*

## The outcome

The attorney who will stand up at the hearing opens one pack and finds everything assembled in the same order every time. The date, time, courtroom, judge and every direction in the notice, in the notice's own words. Every docket entry, as docketed. Every directive in the orders the attorney asked for, quoted with its page and paragraph, footnotes included. Each witness, in the attorney's order, with their declarations or transcripts quoted under the topics the attorney named, every passage cited to paragraph and page or to page and line. Each exhibit with its marking, exchange and stipulation status exactly as the tracker shows it, and each item on the firm's own pre-hearing checklist shown done or open. A separate open-items list gathers what the sources show is still outstanding: a checklist item past its recorded date, a blank in the tracker, a witness with no statement on file, an email a declaration mentions that is not on the exhibit list, an order on the docket nobody supplied.

The gathering arrives done, for the paralegal to check, and the attorney's time goes to the arguments, which only a lawyer can make. The same pack works for a deposition: the witness's earlier statements, the documents and exhibits, and the firm's deposition checklist, with no question outline.

**What it does not do.** It gives no legal advice. It decides nothing about legal meaning, merit, validity, privilege, conflicts or deadlines. It sends, files, signs and calendars nothing. It writes no argument, states no legal standard and makes no strategic assessment; the arguments, the legal standard, the order of proof and every filing are the attorney's. It says nothing about whether a witness is believable or an exhibit admissible, and it never sets one witness's words against another's. It writes no witness list or exhibit list for filing, no stipulation, no letter and no examination questions, even for the attorney to edit. A date the notice or an order states is copied; a date that has to be counted is proposed only under the firm's own counsel-approved rule file, with every day shown, or left uncounted. It names no case, statute or rule from memory, and any that reaches it is marked to be verified by the lawyer and never called good law. Asked what the standard is, whether the firm will win, or whether something is ready to file, it says that is for the named attorney and points to the line of the pack concerned. Everything it prepares is a draft for the named attorney.

## Who starts the work, and when

A paralegal starts it once a hearing is set and the arguing attorney has asked for the pack, naming the witnesses, the topics for each and the orders to include. It runs again whenever the docket, the tracker or the checklist changes before the hearing. The arguing attorney reviews it. In a company with no lawyer of its own, the pack is prepared for the outside counsel who will argue the hearing and goes to that counsel, never to the business as an answer.

It is not for a hearing that has not been set, and it is not for planning a board meeting or a company event: [board meeting pack](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/board-meeting-pack/) and [event plan](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/event-plan/) do that work.

## What they supply

- **The attorney's request:** the matter and case number, the arguing attorney, the notice that sets the hearing, the witnesses in the attorney's order with the topics for each, the orders to include and today's date.
- **The notice setting the hearing,** every page. For a deposition, the notice of deposition.
- **The docket,** exported with the columns Entry, Date Filed and Docket Text, or the docket sheet itself.
- **The orders the attorney listed,** every page.
- **Each witness's prior statements:** declarations and affidavits with numbered paragraphs, and transcripts with their page and line numbers.
- **The exhibit tracker,** exported with the columns Exhibit, Description, Bates Range, Offered By, Witness, Pre-marked, Exchanged and Stipulation.
- **The firm's own pre-hearing checklist,** exported with the columns ID, Item, Owner, Due, Status, Done On and Notes.
- **The firm's deadline rule file,** if it keeps one, approved by an attorney, with the court-closure list.

If an export's column headers differ, the workflow template stops and asks which column is which. It brings no checklist and no court rules of its own, and it uses nothing the firm did not supply: nothing remembered, nothing from the internet.

## What comes out

One draft pack, headed for the named attorney to review, in a fixed order:

- **Hearing:** each detail in the notice's words, with its cite, and any later docket entry that reads as moving it.
- **Procedural history:** every docket entry, as docketed.
- **Orders:** each directive quoted with page and paragraph, and any other order on the docket that was not supplied.
- **Witnesses:** each statement listed, passages quoted under the attorney's topics, and the documents each statement refers to, checked against the tracker.
- **Exhibits:** every field as the tracker records it, with gaps in the numbering noted.
- **Checklist:** every item done or open as the firm records it.
- **Dates:** each one copied, proposed under a named rule with the working shown, or marked not computed.
- **Attorney to confirm:** a line for each part of the pack and each date.
- **Check:** what was read and how.

Then the **open-items list**, in a fixed order, each item with its source and its owner as recorded, and no recommendation. If a page is missing or a later docket entry may have moved the hearing, the pack says so at the top in capitals.

## What has to change for your company

The checklist is yours, and so is the rule file. Write the pre-hearing and deposition checklists the firm actually uses, with owners and due dates, because the workflow template adds no items of its own. A supervising attorney decides which kinds of count staff may propose and keeps the closure list for each court; anything the file leaves out stays uncounted. Map your docket, tracker and checklist exports to the column names once. Keep standing topics for the kinds of hearing you see most, add the words your attorneys never want in a pack, and decide who checks the cites and recounts the dates. The attorney confirms that the firm's policy and any protective order allow the material to be used in the AI product you have approved.

We sit with the paralegal who builds the packs and the attorneys who argue from them, on hearings they already know well, and set it up in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini. Your firm keeps control of how the work gets done.

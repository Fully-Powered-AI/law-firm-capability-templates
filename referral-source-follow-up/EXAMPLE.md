# Referral source follow-up: an invented example

*Illustrative example with invented data.* The firms, the people, the clients, the dates and the materials below are all invented, and the names are made up for this example. Firm A is an invented 35-lawyer business law firm. Elena is a corporate partner there and Sam is an associate on her team. Marcus is a partner at Accounting Firm B, an invented accounting practice that has sent Firm A work before. First come the inputs Elena supplied, in full, then the follow-up the capability returned.

## The inputs

### 1. Settings

```
Partner: Elena, corporate partner, Firm A
Sign-off: Best wishes, Elena
Today: Wednesday 4 November 2026
Follow-up window: 2 working days, Monday to Friday
Word limit: 180
Advertising checklist: the seven default categories
Required label: none
Next step chosen: propose a call once his list of businesses arrives
```

### 2. Contact record (one row from the firm's contact list)

```

```

### 5. Elena's notes

```
Lunch with Marcus (Accounting Firm B), Tuesday 3 November 2026. Elena and Sam from us.

- Marcus said the share sale we did for Client C went well and Client C is pleased. (Client C is his client too.)
- Four of his firm's clients are thinking about selling their businesses in 2027. He wants to know who to send them to.
- I said I'd send him our sale-readiness checklist and the corporate team one-pager.
- He asked how many sales we'd done this year. Said I'd get him the number.
- He asked whether earn-outs are taxed differently after this year's changes. Told him I'd rather not answer off the cuff.
- Marcus will send me a profile of the businesses (rough sizes and sectors, no names) by end of next week.
- I said I'd introduce him to Priya at Bank D, who does acquisition lending, once I've checked with her.
- Sam will send him the date of our January succession planning seminar once it's fixed.
- Maybe a joint session for his clients in the spring?
- Suggest a call when his list comes in.
- Not public: Accounting Firm B is merging with another practice in the spring. Don't mention.
- His daughter starts university next year; he's nervous about it.
```

## What the example shows

- The checklist was the only material that could go: the one-pager's approval had ended, and the clients' briefing was approved for another audience and was never promised.
- Three of Elena's own promises wait for her, each with its reason, instead of being quietly dropped or quietly kept.
- The tax question, the merger, the client's sale, the daughter and the banker's name stay out of the message and the log line. So does Marcus's remark that four of his clients may sell: it is about his clients, and the capability holds back what a contact says about their own clients' plans even when no client is named.
- The "maybe" about a spring session is offered as an option, not written in as a commitment.

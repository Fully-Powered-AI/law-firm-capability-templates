# Discovery requests and commitments register: an invented example

*Illustrative example with invented data.* Ashgrove Vell LLP, Strand Merrow LLP, their people, Tessaly Boatworks Ltd, Ormond Vale Coatings Inc., the District Court of Northmarch, the case, the requests, the responses, the production log, the correspondence below and the rule it cites are all invented. No real court, firm, company, person, case or rule is named.

The paralegal's five inputs come first, in full, then what the workflow template prepares. Page breaks in the served papers are shown as `[page n]`.

Four points the example shows. The promise to produce the supplier communications appears twice, once in the attorney's own note of the call and once in the other side's email, and each is its own line, so the attorney can see which one the other side put in writing. And the call note records that a shorter period for Request No. 4 was agreed, while the other side's email says it was not: the register quotes both and leaves the question to the attorney. Each overdue line in section D names OVC-VOL003, the production whose cover email names no request, so an attorney reading the overdue list alone still sees it, without the register saying whether it is what was promised. And the rule cited in the 10 April email is quoted as written, marked to be verified by the lawyer and given its own line, never checked or relied on.

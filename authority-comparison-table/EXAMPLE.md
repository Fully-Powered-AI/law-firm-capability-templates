## Input 3: text A1

```
A1

CALDERRA COMMERCIAL TENANCY ACT

Section 14. Default and forfeiture.

(a) A lease may provide for forfeiture of the tenancy when rent is not paid
when due.

(b) A landlord shall not declare a forfeiture for nonpayment of rent unless
the landlord has first given the tenant written notice of the default,
delivered personally or by certified mail to the tenant's address for
notices stated in the lease, and the default has continued for ten days
after delivery.

(c) Any provision of a lease that waives the requirements of subsection (b)
is void.

History: Enacted 2011, ch. 88. Amended 2023, ch. 41, effective January 1,
2024.
```

## Input 4: text A2

```
A2

COURT OF APPEALS OF THE STATE OF CALDERRA
SECOND APPELLATE DISTRICT

PELLARY TOOLS, INC., Plaintiff and Appellant,
v.
DENHOLT ROW LLC, Defendant and Respondent.

No. 24-0117
Filed March 4, 2025

Appeal from a judgment of the Superior Court of Calderra, County of
Brenmoor, after a bench trial.

Opinion by Judge Asheford, in which Judge Lindqvist joins. Dissenting
opinion by Judge Corvane.

¶1 Denholt Row LLC leased warehouse space to Pellary Tools, Inc. When
Pellary fell behind on rent, Denholt Row sent a notice of default by email
and, eleven days later, declared the lease forfeited. Pellary sued, and the
trial court entered judgment for Denholt Row. Pellary appeals.

¶2 The question presented is whether a notice of default sent by email
satisfies section 14(b) of the Commercial Tenancy Act.

¶3 The lease named a street address for notices. It did not name an email
address. Pellary's operations manager read the email the day it was sent.

¶4 Section 14(b) requires written notice of the default "delivered
personally or by certified mail to the tenant's address for notices stated
in the lease."

¶5 The statute names two methods of delivery. Email is not one of them.
When the legislature lists the means by which a thing must be done, we do
not add a means it left out.

¶6 Denholt Row argues that Pellary actually received the notice, so the
purpose of the statute was served. Actual receipt does not cure a delivery
method the statute does not allow. Section 14(c) makes void any lease term
that waives subsection (b), and a landlord cannot achieve by practice what
the parties could not achieve by contract.

¶7 The trial court relied on Brisketh Fabrication Co. v. Quellan Estates,
Calderra Super. Ct. No. CV-21-3390 (2022), which treated an emailed notice
as sufficient where the tenant admitted receipt. A trial court decision
does not bind this court, and we do not follow it.

¶8 The Court of Appeals of the State of Pallmoor reached a different result
under a similar statute in Tamsark Logistics LLC v. Orvane Yard Partners
(2024). We are not persuaded. The Pallmoor statute permits delivery "by any
means reasonably calculated to give actual notice," words our legislature
did not use.

¶9 We hold that a notice of default sent only by email does not satisfy
section 14(b).

¶10 The judgment is reversed, and the matter is remanded for further
proceedings consistent with this opinion.

DISSENTING OPINION OF JUDGE CORVANE

¶11 I would affirm.

¶12 Section 14(b) exists so that a tenant learns of a default before losing
its tenancy. Pellary learned of the default the day the email was sent, and
its manager said so at trial.

¶13 The majority reads the words "personally or by certified mail" as the
only permitted methods. The statute does not say "only." Where the tenant
admits actual receipt of a written notice, I would hold that the notice was
delivered.

¶14 The majority's rule lets a tenant that read the notice on the first day
keep the premises without paying, because of the envelope the notice did
not come in. I respectfully dissent.
```

```
A3

COURT OF APPEALS OF THE STATE OF PALLMOOR

TAMSARK LOGISTICS LLC v. ORVANE YARD PARTNERS

No. 23-CA-0815
Filed September 12, 2024

[Excerpt supplied: paragraphs 18 to 21 only]

¶18 The Pallmoor Commercial Leases Act, section 9(2), requires notice of a
default "by any means reasonably calculated to give actual notice to the
tenant."

¶19 Orvane Yard sent its notice by email to the address Tamsark had used for
every rent invoice in the preceding three years. Tamsark's controller
replied to the email the same afternoon.

¶20 A notice that the tenant has actually received and answered has, by
definition, been given by a means reasonably calculated to give actual
notice.

¶21 We conclude that the emailed notice satisfied section 9(2).
```

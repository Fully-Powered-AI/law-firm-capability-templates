# Authority comparison table

From the opinions, statutes and regulations counsel selected, and only those, the researching lawyer gets one side-by-side table: each authority's court, date, stated issue, operative words, posture and separate opinions, quoted with pinpoints, and a list of every quotation to check. The lawyer decides what applies.

*Workflow template · Law firms · Legal and contracts · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/authority-comparison-table/)*

## The outcome

The researching lawyer starts the close reading with the whole set laid out in one place. Every authority sits in the same fixed fields, each filled with the text's own words and the exact paragraph, page or section they came from. Counsel's own points run across the set, so the passages on each point line up side by side. The opinion printed first sits beside any dissent or concurrence, sentence against sentence, and neither is labelled the majority. Every place one opinion in the set mentions another is pulled out, and every authority the texts mention but nobody supplied is listed, unread, for the lawyer to chase. The draft ends with a numbered list of every quotation, ready to check against the official source.

This is preparation for a lawyer, and it stays preparation. It gives no legal advice. It decides nothing about legal meaning, merit, validity, privilege, conflicts or deadlines: it never says which authority applies, which sentence is the holding, or how two authorities differ, and it never calls one current, binding or good law. It counts no date: a period in a statute is quoted, never turned into a deadline. It writes nothing for use, not a memo, brief section, letter or notice, even for the lawyer to edit. It adds no authority, names none from memory, and treats an authority missing from the set as unknown, never as proof that none exists. It gives no view on whether the draft or anything else is ready to sign, send or rely on: asked for a verdict, a date, a case or what to do, it says only that this is for the named lawyer, names the lawyer and points to one line of the draft. It sends, files, signs and calendars nothing.

## Who starts the work, and when

Once the authorities for a defined question have been chosen and their texts gathered, the researching lawyer runs it, or hands it to a research librarian or paralegal, before the close reading and the writing begin. Whoever runs it, the draft goes to a lawyer by name. At a firm that is the researching lawyer; at a company it is in-house counsel, and where a company has no lawyer of its own, the outside counsel it instructs. A business never receives it as an answer.

It is not for finding or choosing authorities (a [research plan for counsel](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/research-plan-for-counsel/) comes before it), for reports and articles, or for checking the citations and quotations in a finished draft ([citation and quotation check](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/citation-and-quotation-check/) prepares that check). Neither workflow template says whether an authority is still good law; the lawyer checks that through the firm's own citation checks.

## What they supply

- **The research question,** in counsel's own words.
- **The scope counsel set:** the jurisdiction by name and the date range for decisions.
- **The set list,** one row per authority, with the columns ID, Citation as supplied, Kind (opinion, statute or regulation), Text supplied (full or an excerpt) and an optional note from counsel. If a column is named differently, the workflow template stops and asks which is which.
- **The texts,** one per row, exactly as counsel selected them.
- **Counsel's points to compare,** if any, each with the exact terms that mark it.
- **The names:** the matter, the researching lawyer and the responsible attorney.

The workflow template ships no authorities and uses none from memory. Whatever is not in the set stays out of the table.

## What comes out

One draft, headed as a draft for the researching lawyer, in nine fixed sections:

- **The set at a glance:** each authority's citation as supplied, its court or source and date as printed, whether its caption names counsel's jurisdiction, whether a decision falls inside counsel's date range, and whether the text is full or an excerpt.
- **Authority by authority:** the issue as the text states it, the operative text counsel named or the lead opinion's stated conclusion, the procedural posture and the disposition, each quoted with a pinpoint, or marked as not in the text supplied.
- **Side by side on counsel's points:** every sentence that holds counsel's terms, authority by authority.
- **Lead and separate opinions:** the opinion printed first and any dissent or concurrence, named as the text names them and placed next to each other on each point, with every sentence where one mentions the other.
- **Cross-references within the set:** each paragraph where one opinion names another in the set, with any signal words it holds, such as "not persuaded", copied as printed, and the points on which more than one authority uses counsel's terms. It says nothing about agreement or conflict; the lawyer decides.
- **Authorities mentioned but not supplied,** copied as cited and marked to be verified by the lawyer.
- **Quotations to check,** each numbered with its place.
- **For the lawyer to confirm:** a line for each authority's text and citation, each authority whose caption lacks counsel's jurisdiction name or whose date falls outside counsel's range, any part of the scope counsel left out, the quotations, any counted pinpoint, the unread authorities and the set itself.
- **A check block** of what was read and what the draft does not say.

## What has to change for your company

Your lawyers set the lists the workflow template works from: the phrases that mark an issue or a stated conclusion, the words that mark a disposition or a posture, and the signal words listed beside each cross-reference. Decide which printed numbers your lawyers want used as pinpoints, and how a pinpoint counted by hand is marked. Name who runs it for each lawyer, where the reviewed draft is kept with the matter's research record, and who may see it. Confirm under your own policy that a matter's research texts may be used in the AI product you have approved.

We sit with the lawyers and librarians who lay out a research set today, write down how they really do it, and set it up in the AI product your firm or company has approved: ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini. Your team keeps control of how the work gets done.

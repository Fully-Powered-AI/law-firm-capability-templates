# Court-order deadline notice preparation: an invented example

*Illustrative example with invented data.* Tallis Marrow LLP, its people, its client, the Superior Court of Eastmere, the order, the rule file and the court-closure list below are all invented. The rule file is one invented firm’s way of counting for an invented court. It is not any real court’s rule and must not be used for a real deadline.

The legal assistant’s four inputs come first, in full, then what the capability prepares.

## Input 1: the request

Sent by Dalia Renner, legal assistant, on Monday 9 November 2026.

```
Copperline: the order on the discovery dispute came in Friday. It is saved to
the matter file. Please prepare the order notice.

Matter: Copperline Bakery Supply Co. v. Ostrander Freight, Inc.,
case no. LC-CV-26-01873. We act for the plaintiff.

On the matter: Marcus Thibault, handling attorney; Helen Voss, supervising
partner; Joan Abara, paralegal; Petra Lindahl, docketing.

Attached: the order (3 pages), our order deadline rule file, the court-closure
list.
```

## Input 3: the firm’s order deadline rule file

```
Tallis Marrow LLP · Order deadline rule file
Dated: 1 October 2026        Review by: 31 March 2027
Approved by: Helen Voss, partner. Changes go through her.
Covers: civil orders of the Superior Court of Eastmere. No other court.

R-0. A date the order states ("no later than", "on or before", "by", a hearing
     date). Copy it. Never recount it.

R-1. A count of calendar days from entry of the order.
     Phrases: "within N days of entry of this order", "within N days of entry",
     "within N days of this order", "N days after entry".
     Start: the date on the court's ENTERED stamp on page 1. Never the date the
     judge signed.
     Count calendar days. The day of entry is not counted.
     If the last day is a Saturday, a Sunday or a day on the court-closure
     list, move forward to the next day that is none of these.

Not covered. Staff propose no date for any of these; they go to the handling
attorney:
     - a count from service of the order;
     - a count backwards from a hearing, a conference or trial;
     - a count in court days or business days;
     - a count from the date the order was signed;
     - a count from an event the order does not date;
     - a sentence that holds two kinds of count, even for two different tasks:
       neither count gets a proposed date;
     - anything this file does not list.

Every proposed date is confirmed by the handling attorney before docketing
calendars it. If the handling attorney is away, the supervising partner
confirms.
```

## What the capability prepares

A short note to the legal assistant, then the draft.

> Dalia, the draft notice for Copperline is below. I read 3 of 3 pages. No stop applied to the whole order. Two dates are proposed under your rule R-1. Three counts are not computed. Your rule file does not cover a count from service (the payment). It does not cover a count in court days or a count backwards from a conference (the joint statement). And the 14 days for the meet and confer sit in the same sentence as that court-day count: your rule file sends a sentence that holds two kinds of count to the attorney, so I have proposed no date for the meet and confer either. Two directives are outside the numbered list: one in footnote 1 on page 1 and one in the discussion on page 2.
>
> Still to do, by people: read the draft against the order, recount the two proposed dates on a calendar, and give the draft to Marcus Thibault to confirm. I have sent nothing and calendared nothing. The notice does not go to the matter team, and docketing calendars nothing, until he confirms.

The meet and confer is the line to notice. Taken alone, "within 14 days of this order" is wording rule R-1 lists. It gets no proposed date because the court put it in one sentence with a count in court days before a conference, and the firm's rule file sends such a sentence to the attorney whole. The capability stops there and says why.

Marcus Thibault confirms or corrects each line and supplies the dates for B, C and F. Only then does Dalia Renner circulate the notice, and only then does Petra Lindahl take the 10 December conference off the calendar and add the confirmed dates.

Written by Fully Powered AI in its own words. One published method shaped it. CaseMark/skills (Apache-2.0), in its court formatting method, supplied the idea of gathering the court, the case number, the kind of document and the judge's own requirements before anything is looked at; of letting the judge's and the court's own requirements outrank any general practice; of holding every part of the caption against the court's docket and allowing no shortened party name; of a closing pass over length limits, certificates, exhibit labels and references, and the signature block; and of asking which requirement is meant when one is unclear, rather than guessing. Fully Powered AI added the rest: no formatting defaults of any kind, so the firm's own attorney-approved checklist for that court and judge is the only measure; a pass or open result for every checklist item, the requirement quoted and what the files show located by file, page and line, and each open item given its kind; anything the text cannot show, such as type size, left open for a person to check by eye; words never counted by the assistant; exhibits matched by label in both directions, never by what they seem to be; the proof of service compared with the matter's service list and with the counsel the docket shows, without saying who must be served; the attorney's details compared with the firm's own roster and a signature line that must stay blank; a draft addressed to the attorney who signs, with a line to confirm for every open item; and the stops and refusals: no court rule from memory, no date counted, nothing written for the package, no file changed, and nothing signed, filed or served. No third-party text is reused.

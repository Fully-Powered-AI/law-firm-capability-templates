# Deposition transcript summary

Before the next deposition, a motion or trial, the attorney has a draft page-line summary of the transcript, arranged by the topics they named: every point cited, every exhibit and objection logged, and every place the testimony differs flagged without a label. The attorney decides what it means.

*Workflow template · Law firms · Legal and contracts · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/deposition-transcript-summary/)*

## The outcome

The attorney sits down to prepare with the witness's whole testimony already sorted under the topics that matter to the case. Every point carries its page and lines, so any point can be found in the transcript straight away. Where the witness gave two different times, a date that differs from a document, or an answer the errata sheet changes, both places are quoted together, each with its cite. Every objection is logged with what followed it, every exhibit with where it was marked, and a follow-up list shows what the record says is still outstanding: documents named in testimony that are not exhibits and were not supplied, things the witness offered to provide, questions left unanswered. The first read is prepared for the paralegal to check and the attorney to review, and the attorney's time goes to the judgment only a lawyer can make.

**What it does not do.** It gives no legal advice. It decides nothing about legal meaning, merit, validity, privilege, conflicts or deadlines. It sends, files, signs and calendars nothing. It quotes and locates; it never characterises. Nothing is called an admission or evasive, and the summary never says which of two passages is right. An instruction not to answer is recorded, never assessed. It writes no cross-examination questions, no brief section, no letter and no filing, even for the attorney to edit. It names no case, statute or rule from memory. If one reaches it, whether the attorney gave it, someone passed it on, or it is cited in the transcript or a document, it is shown only in the words it came in, flagged for the lawyer to verify, and never described as current, valid or good law. Asked whether the draft is ready to sign, what testimony means, when something is due, what a claim is worth or what to do, it says only that this is for the named attorney and points to the line of the draft concerned. Everything it prepares is a draft for the named attorney.

## Who starts the work, and when

A paralegal or associate starts it when the transcript arrives with its page and line numbers, once the attorney has named the topics to read it for. The attorney who will use it, usually the handling attorney preparing for the next deposition, a motion or trial, reviews it. In a company with no lawyer of its own, the summary is prepared for the outside counsel on the case and goes to that counsel, never to the business as an answer.

It is not for notes of a meeting or an interview that was not under oath: [meeting agenda, notes and follow-ups](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/meeting-agenda-notes-and-follow-ups/) and [customer interview synthesis](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/customer-interview-synthesis/) do that work.

## What they supply

- **The transcript**, every page, with the cover, appearances, index and reporter's certificate, with every page and line number still on it.
- **The attorney's request:** the matter, the case number, the witness, the attorney's name and the topics, in the attorney's order.
- **The errata sheet,** if the witness made one.
- **Documents to compare with,** such as the exhibits, each with a Bates number or page number.
- **Other transcripts to compare with,** if the attorney wants them, with their page and line numbers.

The workflow template compares the testimony with nothing else: nothing remembered, nothing from the internet, nothing from another matter.

## What comes out

One draft, headed for the named attorney to review, in a fixed order:

- **Proceeding:** court, caption, date, counsel, reporter and how the deposition ended, each with its cite.
- **Testimony by topic:** each question in plain words with the witness's answer quoted, its page and lines, and a neutral flag where it differs from another place, such as "Differs from p. 6, ll. 7–8".
- **Differences:** each one with both places quoted and cited and the fact they concern, including every change on the errata sheet.
- **Objections:** each with its words, the question and what followed: answered, withdrawn, rephrased or no answer.
- **Exhibits:** each with its description, Bates range, where it was marked and whether the index agrees.
- **Follow-up list:** what the record shows is outstanding, with the passages, and no recommendation.
- **Transcript condition:** pages read, anything missing or unreadable, reporter's markers, and whether the copy is certified.
- **Attorney to confirm:** a line for the topics, each difference, the objections, the exhibits, the follow-up list, the transcript's condition and the cite check.

If a page is missing or the copy is a rough draft, the draft says so at the top in capitals. If the transcript has no page and line numbers, or no topics were given, nothing is prepared until they are.

## What has to change for your company

The topics are the attorney's, and they are the heart of this. Many teams keep a standing list for each kind of case, which the attorney edits for each witness. Choose your cite style once, add the words your attorneys never want to see in a summary, decide who checks the cites and how many, and say where the draft is kept in the matter file. The attorney confirms that the firm's policy and any protective order allow transcripts and exhibits to be used in the AI product you have approved.

We sit with the paralegal who prepares the summaries and the attorney who uses them, on transcripts they already know well, and set it up in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini. Your firm keeps control of how the work gets done.

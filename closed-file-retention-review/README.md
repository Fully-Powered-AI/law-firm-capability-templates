# Closed-file retention review

When closed matter files are ready for records review, the records officer has a draft list for supervising counsel in four groups: files whose period has run under the firm's own schedule, files under a hold, client originals not yet returned, and files that cannot be assessed. Nothing is destroyed or moved.

*Workflow template · Law firms · Legal and contracts · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/closed-file-retention-review/)*

## The outcome

Supervising counsel opens one list and sees exactly where a batch of closed files stands. Files whose retention period has run under the firm's own approved schedule are together, each with its end date and the counting shown. Every file a hold covers is in its own group, whatever its age, so nobody has to remember which client is under a hold. Original client documents the log does not show as returned are listed with every file of that matter, so a deed or a signed original is in front of counsel before anything happens to that matter's files. And every file the records cannot settle, because the closing date is unclear, nobody recorded who authorised the closure, or the schedule sends it to counsel, is named with what is needed and from whom.

This is preparation for a lawyer. What it does not do: it gives no legal advice. It decides nothing about legal meaning, merit, validity, privilege, conflicts or deadlines. It sends, files, signs and calendars nothing. It states no retention period of its own and names no law or rule: every end date is a proposed date, counted under the firm's own schedule with the working shown. It lists no disposal of any file under a hold, and the group whose period has run is not a list of files to destroy. It destroys, moves, returns and changes nothing. Each review is headed as a draft for supervising counsel by name, with a line for each point counsel confirms or decides.

## Who starts the work, and when

The records officer starts it when a batch of closed files is ready for records review: a run of storage boxes, a range of electronic folders, or the files closed in a given period. Supervising counsel, the lawyer the firm names to decide on closed files, reviews it and decides.

It is the later step after [matter closing checklist preparation](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/matter-closing-checklist-preparation/), which closes each matter and deliberately sets no retention period. It is not for closing a matter, for writing to a client about returning originals, or for deciding anything about a hold: the lawyer who issued the hold decides its scope and its release, and [legal hold custodian tracker](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/legal-hold-custodian-tracker/) keeps track of it while it runs. Asked whether a file can be shredded, how long the law says to keep something, or to mark files for destruction, it gives one fixed reply: that is for supervising counsel, named, with a pointer to the line of the review the question is about.

## What they supply

- **The request:** the batch, the number of files the records system says it holds, supervising counsel's name, where counsel records decisions, the review date, and when the hold register and the originals log were exported.
- **The closed-file inventory:** one row per box or electronic folder, with its matter, client, record class, location, closing date and who authorised the closure.
- **The firm's retention schedule,** approved by its own attorney, dated and in date: the record classes with the period for each, and a written counting rule that says what a period runs from, how its end date is set and when it counts as run.
- **The hold register:** each hold with the client and matters it covers, any scope note, and whether it is active or released, by whom and when.
- **The return-of-originals log:** each original client document received, and when and to whom it went back.

Default column headers are set out in the template; if yours differ, the workflow template asks which column is which instead of guessing. The template ships no schedule and no periods. Without the firm's approved schedule, or with one past its review date or missing its counting rule, it compares nothing.

## What comes out

One draft review in six parts, headed for supervising counsel by name, with a short note to the records officer below it:

- **Retention period has run under the schedule:** files with nothing else recorded against them, each with its proposed end date and working. For counsel to decide; not a disposal recommendation.
- **Under a hold:** every file an active hold covers, with the hold, how it matched and its scope note copied word for word.
- **Original client documents not yet returned:** each item from the log with no recorded return or no recipient, with every file of its matter in the batch.
- **Cannot be assessed:** files with an unclear closing date, no recorded closure authorisation, a class the schedule does not list or counts from an event, each with what is needed and from whom.
- **Counsel to decide and confirm:** a line for every proposed end date, every file in the four groups, a question on how far each active hold reaches, a line for counsel to verify any authority found in the inputs, the hold register being current, and every file being accounted for.
- **Check:** files read against the stated count, how every file is accounted for, the schedule and exports used, and any text in the inputs that was copied rather than acted on.

The records officer checks each entry against the box or folder, recounts each end date and gets what the fourth group needs. Supervising counsel decides each point and records it in the firm's own log. Anything returned, kept or disposed of afterwards is done by people, under the firm's own procedure.

It is a different piece of work from [obsolete copy check](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/obsolete-copy-check/), which finds out-of-date controlled copies in use, and from [shared folder filing plan](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/shared-folder-filing-plan/), which plans how a working folder is tidied.

## What has to change for your company

The retention schedule is yours, and it is the heart of this. The attorney who owns it sets the record classes, the period for each, which classes are permanent or counted from an event, and the counting rule: what a period runs from, how its end date is set, when it counts as run and what happens to a 29 February closing date. You choose how a batch is picked, map your column names if your records system, hold register or originals log differs, decide whether a return needs a recipient's name or a signed receipt as well, and name where counsel records decisions and who decides when counsel is away.

We sit with your records officer and the attorney who owns the schedule, write down how closed-file reviews are really run, and set it up in the AI product your firm has approved: ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini. Your firm keeps control of how the work gets done.
